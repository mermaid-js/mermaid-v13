# D746: A C4 relation's tag keeps off boundary borders and headers, even when moved by the author

A relation's label (its edge-label tag in SVG output, its framed text in text output) treats a boundary's frame border as cover, as it already treated a boundary's header (the label, `[type]` and description): the placement slides it to a spot clear of both. This holds for a tag that `UpdateRelStyle`'s `$offsetX`/`$offsetY` put on a border or a header, too: the offsets give the spot the search starts from, not one it must keep (#1363: the "Buys from" tag of `e2e/diagrams/c4/baseline/styles/update-styles.mmd` sat on the dashed border of the `Shop` boundary).

A tag does not float far from its line to clear a border. When no spot clear of the borders is found, or the clear spot lies farther from the line, by more than half the tag's smaller side, than the spot found with the borders left out, the tag takes that spot, across the border, and is marked `crowded`, as a tag with no clear spot is (D455). This keeps a tag on its line where the line crosses a gap between two frames narrower than the tag (`nightly-check/docs/5-deployment`, "Replicates data to"), or runs a short way from a frame's side to a shape inside it (`pr-check/repeated-relations-room`, Bank to Vault).

v12 drew a label where its offsets put it, whatever it covered. The layout invariants (`c4LayoutViolations`) now report a label that is not crowded and covers a boundary header or lies across a frame border.

Out of scope: a relation line that runs through a boundary header (#997) and the lanes the router picks (#1177).

Refines D455 and D679.

(Knut, 2026-11-27, spec #1342.)
