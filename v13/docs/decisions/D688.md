# D688: Contrast ink judges against the author's fill under a hand-drawn hachure

Under `look: handDrawn`, contrast ink (R-THEME-12, D21) still picks black or white against the colour of the author's `style` or `classDef` fill. It does this even though the hand-drawn plugin draws that fill as hachure strokes with gaps between them. This settles the open point of decision 6 in spec #1163. Nothing changes.

The measurement is in #1178 (ticket #1171), taken in Chromium at the default theme:

- **Subgraph titles.** A 3 px hachure stroke every 2 px covers 99.7 to 100% of the title box. The fill is in effect solid there.
- **Node labels.** A 1.5 px stroke every 2 px covers about 87% of the label box, so about 13% of the background shows through.
- **#1155's fill (`#a34`).** White text reaches 6.4:1 against the strokes. Against the light-weighted average of the box it reaches 4.2:1, just under AA.
- **Dark fills** (`#222222`, `#622`, `#1f4e79`). White still meets AA against that average.

Why it stays as it is:

- **The theme ink is worse on every fill measured.** On `#a34` it reaches 3.5:1 against the same average, and it is worse still on the dark fills. Turning contrast ink off under hachure would make labels less readable, not more.
- **The alternative needs a new contract.** Choosing the ink against a coverage-weighted colour would flip `#a34` and `#2e7d32` from white to black. But core would have to learn the hachure coverage from the hand-drawn plugin. A diagram package must not check `look` to find it (D152), so this would be a new contract between core and the plugin, for borderline gains on a few mid-dark fills.

Knut: "Keep as is".

(Knut, 2026-11-23, spec #1163, decision 6.)
