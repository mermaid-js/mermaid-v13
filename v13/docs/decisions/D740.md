# D740: A packet row grows to fit its tallest label; amends D343

A packet row is as tall as `max(rowHeight, its tallest label)`, the label measured in the font it is drawn in. Every block of the row takes that height, and every label stays centred on its block. Rows are stacked: each starts `pY` below the bottom of the row above, so the rows below a grown row, the canvas height, the title and the bounds move down by what it grew. The bit numbers stay `bitNumberGap` above their own row.

A label shrinks only to fit its block's width, down to the bit-number size, then overflows the width, as D343 said. Its height never shrinks it, so a label whose width fits keeps `packetLabelFontSize`. A label that shrank for its width grows the row only by the height it has at that size.

The cause is D343's height fit from #774: v12 printed `<br>` literally, so every label was one line. In v13 a label of three lines shrank to 10 px to fit the 32 px row, and one of four lines overflowed onto the border and the bit numbers of the next row (#1267).

Rows of one-line labels are not taller than one line at the default config and theme, so they keep v12's positions byte for byte. A `rowHeight` set lower than one line of the label grows too: v12 drew that label over the border.

The layout invariants (`packages/diagram-packet/src/layout/invariants.ts`) gain two rules: a row is as tall as its tallest label, and no label line is above or below its block. A label may still be wider than its block (D343, #1266).

(Knut, 2026-11-27, spec #1341 T1: accepted as proposed.)
